Model

Equilibrium propagation: relax freely to z⁰, then relax again with a weak pull β·C toward the label to z^β. The weight update is read off the contrast (one-sided — this app shows standard nudging only; the checkpoint was trained with centred ±β EP, see the About tab). For a dense layer it is a difference of outer products, postsynaptic error × presynaptic activity:

ΔW ∝ −[∂E/∂W(z^β) − ∂E/∂W(z⁰)] / β,
∂E/∂W1 = −sᵀ[(u − û) ⊙ (1 − û²)]

What you are looking at

Two layers of 256 tanh units, u and s, joined into a loop: û = tanh(s·W1 + b1) and ŝ = tanh(u·W2 + b2 + x_inj), where x_inj = x·Win + bin embeds the clues (16 cells × 5 one-hot = 80 inputs). The energy E = ½‖u − û‖² + ½‖s − ŝ‖² is zero exactly at a fixed point. Unrolled, the loop is a deep feedforward network s₀ = 0 → u₁ → s₁ → u₂ → … that repeats W1 and W2 and re-injects x_inj into every s layer. The board is read from s by V (256 → 16 cells × 4 digits).

How these weights were trained

These weights were trained by equilibrium propagation (centred, ±β) on gradient-descent-on-E free-phase states, with heavy-ball momentum and a spectral-norm cap on W1/W2 — see the "model" and "summary" rows below for the exact numbers. Inference here is the same gradient descent on E used in training, run live in your browser, started from zero so you can watch it converge (training itself started from one feedforward sweep; both reach the identical equilibrium — see "free-phase init" below). The nudge and the EP signal you can watch in the Learn tab are computed live on these weights too — nothing here is pre-baked.

What is illustrative

All values, predictions, errors, energies, probabilities and EP gradients are computed from the model state. Illustrative choices: the placement of units inside a layer (s units grouped by the board cell their readout weights favour, u units by the s group they feed most), drawing only the strongest few hundred weights, particle density as a visual proxy for |w × activity|, and the geometry of the fold animation.
